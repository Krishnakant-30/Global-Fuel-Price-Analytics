ABOUT THE PROJECT

Making sense of global fuel prices

This project explores how petrol, diesel and LPG prices vary across countries and change over time. It brings fuel prices together with taxation, subsidies, income levels and Brent crude oil to help explain the patterns seen in the data.

01 · PURPOSE

What are we trying to understand?

Identify broad price movements from 2020 to 2026 and compare how fuel costs differ between countries, regions and fuel types.

02 · WHAT WE COMPARE

Prices and possible influences

Petrol, diesel, LPG, Brent crude oil and tax percentages are reviewed alongside subsidy levels and income groups. These factors provide context; they do not by themselves prove cause and effect.

GLOBAL FUEL PRICE ANALYTICS

Project Summary

A data analytics project focused on comparing global fuel prices and exploring taxation, subsidy and crude-oil price trends using Tableau visualizations.

  • Dataset: Global Fuel Prices
  • Time period: 2020–2026
  • Tools: Tableau Public, HTML, CSS, JavaScript and Flask
GLOBAL FUEL PRICES DATASET

Dataset Details

2020–2026

84 countries across multiple regions and income levels.

The dataset contains date, country, region, income level, subsidy level, petrol price, diesel price, LPG price, Brent crude price and tax percentage.

CORE MEASURES

Fuel and Tax Indicators

Compare average petrol, diesel and LPG prices with tax percentages and Brent crude-oil prices.

These measures help explore patterns across countries and over time. Taxation and subsidies are treated as analytical context, not proof that one factor alone causes a price change.

STEP 1

Analysis Workflow — Data Exploration

Dataset review and preparation

  • Review fuel-price records and the available fields.
  • Compare prices across countries, regions and income groups.
  • Analyze changes in fuel prices and taxation over time.
  • Present findings using interactive Tableau visualizations.
STEP 2

Visualization and Interpretation

Tableau dashboard and data story

  • Use maps to compare petrol and diesel prices across countries.
  • Use trend lines to examine petrol, diesel, LPG and Brent crude prices.
  • Compare average tax percentages across regions and countries.
  • Summarize the findings in a dashboard and guided data story.

Note: the included project dataset is for analytical demonstration and should not be treated as verified official market statistics.

Global Trend

CHAPTER 02

Fuel Price Comparison Across Top 3 Countries

Compare average petrol, diesel and LPG prices across the three countries shown in the worksheet.

  • Identify which fuel types have higher or lower prices within each country.
  • Compare the same fuel across the three countries.
  • Notice whether the price gap is similar across petrol, diesel and LPG.
  • Use the worksheet’s labels and units when interpreting differences; do not assume that a higher price alone means a country has higher taxes.

CHAPTER 03

Taxation

Explore how tax percentages vary across the countries or groups included in the worksheet.

  • Compare the tax levels displayed for different countries or regions.
  • Consider how taxes may contribute to the final price paid by consumers.
  • Remember that prices can also be affected by subsidies, distribution costs, currency changes and local policies.
  • Treat the chart as evidence of patterns, not proof that tax is the only cause of a price difference.

CHAPTER 04

Country and Regional Differences

Look beyond the global average to see how fuel prices differ by location and group.

  • Compare countries and regions using the measures available in the worksheet.
  • Check whether petrol, diesel and LPG show similar geographic patterns.
  • Use filters and labels to focus on specific places or categories.
  • Consider regional context before drawing conclusions from a single country or average.

CHAPTER 05

What the comparisons show

Bring the four views together to form a balanced overview of the dataset.

  • Global trends show when average fuel prices change over time.
  • The top-three-country comparison highlights differences between fuel types and selected countries.
  • The taxation view adds policy context to price comparisons.
  • The country and regional view shows why a single global average cannot describe every market.
  • Use the interactive dashboard below to investigate questions raised by these patterns.
CHAPTER 06

Reading the data carefully

A clear story depends on comparing like with like and being transparent about the limits of the data.

  • Check the time period, units, labels and filters before comparing values.
  • Distinguish averages from individual observations.
  • Do not treat correlation as proof of cause and effect.
  • Interpret taxation and subsidies alongside other market factors.
  • The dataset is intended for project analysis and should not be treated as verified official market statistics.
